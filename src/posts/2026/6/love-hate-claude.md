The relationship I have built with Claude over the past few months is genuinely hard to characterize. It is not straightforwardly good or bad. It is something more complicated than either.

## The Love

Claude has accelerated my development work in ways I did not expect. Tasks that would have consumed my evenings now get done in hours. Because I understand the systems and constraints well enough to specify them clearly, the output is usually close to what I need. That compression of effort has given me more headspace for higher-order work - raising standards, shaping how my team approaches problems.

The clearest example is AskAuntieBot. The project had been in my head for over two years. I never started it because I was occupied with other things, and running idle cloud services on a personal project felt unjustifiable. With Claude Code, I built and deployed it to production within two months. That would not have happened otherwise.

Beyond the project work, I have automated a handful of recurring tasks - calendar entries, post rewrites, resume tailoring. Small things, but they compound.

<hr>

## The Hate

The irony is that I am not less busy. I am busier.

Using Claude well requires understanding it. That means time spent reading about LLMs, agents, and how to prompt effectively - on top of the SWE fundamentals I was already working to deepen. Every session can get expensive quickly, especially on complex tasks, so there is real pressure to be deliberate. And after every session comes review work: checking that the output is correct, that the architecture is sound, that nothing was quietly broken elsewhere. That is not free time. That is a different kind of work.

The more uncomfortable cost is skill erosion. I have always found coding genuinely satisfying - building something from scratch, understanding each piece, making it work. Over these months, I have largely stopped writing code myself. I direct instead. I have been running JavaScript and Golang projects without ever really learning either language. The trade-off is real, even if I have made peace with most of it.

My writing is following the same pattern. Claude cleans up my drafts. The drafts I hand over are rougher than they used to be because I know they will be tidied. The muscle weakens.

<hr>

## The Relationship

Whether Claude makes me more productive depends entirely on how you define productive. By the standard metrics - tasks completed, time on delivery - yes. By a more honest measure - am I actually getting better at anything I care about - the answer is less clear.

What I have learned through Claude is partly shaped by it. Some JavaScript, some node.js, more about agents and AI infrastructure. That is not nothing. But the things I most want to develop - deeper SWE knowledge, DevOps competence - are exactly the areas where Claude removes the friction that forces learning. When the path of least resistance is to hand it off, the harder path gets avoided.

The privacy question sits underneath all of this. Getting the most from Claude means giving it context - about projects, workflows, data. That means a service provider has access to information I would not have shared casually. It is a trade-off I have made, but deliberately, not by default.

What Claude forces me to reckon with is not whether the tool is useful - it clearly is - but whether I am using it in a way that compounds over time, or one that quietly hollows out the skills I want to keep.
