> _Who is your inspiration?_

That question used to come up often during my English lessons in primary school. It was neither difficult nor easy to answer. At that age, I was not inspired by anyone or anything. Maybe I was simply too young to understand what inspiration meant. To complete the assignment, I would randomly choose a well known local figure like Robert Kuok or Lim Goh Tong and write about their achievements.

As I grew older, not much changed. I was not inspired by a person or a calling. I was driven by one goal, financial independence. I believed that as long as I worked hard and chased opportunities, things would work out. The problem was that I was moving in too many directions at once and did not achieve anything meaningful for a long time.

That realization was disappointing, but it also marked progress. Compared to a decade ago, I now have clarity. I know that I want to become a strong engineer in the IT field, especially in backend engineering and artificial intelligence, with a greater focus on backend work.

The period when I came to this realization was not easy. It was confusing and frustrating at times. I questioned what I really wanted in the long run. Eventually, I decided to step away from my usual commitments and went on a solo trip. In many ways, it felt like my _Eat Pray Love_ moment.

During that trip, I watched many films that I had always meant to watch but never did. I enjoyed them so much that I continued watching more even after I returned. Over time, I started to understand why those films resonated with me.

Yes, they had strong plots. But more than that, they were carried by actors, actresses, and directors who were deeply committed to their craft. The effort, discipline, and thought they invested in their work were evident. They brought characters to life in a way that felt real and powerful. When a film reaches that level, it is not by accident. It is the result of consistent dedication.

Even actors who did not receive recognition early on, like Jake Gyllenhaal, or those who had to wait years for it, like Leonardo DiCaprio, did not give up. They kept improving. They kept refining their craft. They kept showing up.

That is what inspires me now. Not a single individual, but the mindset behind great filmmaking. The discipline. The persistence. The commitment to excellence.

I want to bring that same standard into my own work. To become better than I was yesterday. To take my craft seriously. To aim for depth, not just progress.

Inspiration does not always come from where you expect it. Sometimes it comes quietly, from observing people who care deeply about what they do.
