My Claude Code usage has been fun the past few months. I was running it through an Amazon Bedrock API key tied to my company, and as I explored the limits of what it could do in my day-to-day work, I unknowingly began to abuse it. There was little to no oversight or guideline on usage, and it quietly racked up an average monthly cost of over a thousand dollars.

To the company, that might be a rounding error. But it was for one user. Extend an unlimited budget like that to everyone and the cost curve gets ugly fast. The bigger problem wasn't the bill, though. Unlimited usage eroded my own cognitive capability and robbed me of learning opportunities, and that started something closer to an identity crisis.

<hr>

I could develop and ship a project in under a week, prototype to done. But I had little understanding of what I'd actually built. The AI coding assistant took over the entire coding portion. I defined specifications, constraints, and deliverables, and whenever something needed to change, I just prompted it to change. Was the change fast? Yes. Did it follow best practices? Not necessarily. Did it work in the end? Yes, after enough tweaks.

That wasn't fun. I went through a real identity crisis questioning what I could actually contribute, what I could still learn, and whether I was growing technically at all. My mind felt like it was rotting, having handed everything over to the assistant. Then I ran into a quote: *are you climbing the mountain for the world to see you, or for you to see the world?*

It's a profound question, and it hit hard. It asks whether you're driven by recognition and external validation, or by personal growth, clarity, and pushing past your own comfort zone.

<hr>

It applies to a lot of areas of my life, but in this case it forced a specific question: was I using the AI coding assistant to deliver products for stakeholders and collect external validation, or was I using it to learn and grow technically myself?

I'm the latter, not the former. I'd gone through a couple of interviews recently where the interviewers challenged my technical understanding hard. I thought I could get by on the strength of what I'd shipped using AI assistants, but the assistants weren't there to help me in the room. I had to rely on my own brain, got mentally challenged, and lost more than I won. But I felt joy in it. The interviewers were pushing me past my current capability, and that mattered more than winning.

That's where I want to be again.

<hr>

This is a long way of saying I've stopped using the Amazon Bedrock API key to power Claude Code. I'm switching to my own subscription and relearning how to use it properly, now that there are real constraints and guardrails in place. 

I don't know how this settles yet, whether it lands back where I was or somewhere more deliberate. I'll find out the same way I used to: by doing the work myself.
